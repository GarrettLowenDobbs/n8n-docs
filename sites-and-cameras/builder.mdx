Builder is where you configure the real-world spatial context of a site, cameras, calibration, points of interest, geofences, facilities, floor plans, and regions, so Worlds can translate what your cameras see into real-world locations. Opening **Sites** takes you straight into Builder for a site, where you can see and edit everything in one place. It's meant to be built up iteratively: a site is usable as soon as cameras are placed and streaming, and you can layer in calibration, points of interest, geofences, and facilities over time as needed.

## Before You Start

- Sites & Cameras requires the Manager role or above. Managers can open Builder to view a site, but editing requires the Administrator role. If you can only view, Builder shows **You have view-only access to this site.**
- You don't need to complete every object type at once. Start with cameras, then add calibration, points of interest, geofences, and facilities as your use cases call for them.
- Changes you make on the map, like dragging a camera or reshaping a geofence, save automatically. The status in the top right shows **Saving…** or **Saved**, and **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, or Cmd on a Mac) step back through your map edits. Forms in the right panel still have their own **Save** button.

## Step 1: Select or Create a Site

Under **Sites & Cameras > Sites**, Builder opens on your first favorite site, or your first site by name. Click the site name in the top left to switch sites: search by name, star any site to keep it under **Favorites**, or click **New Site** at the bottom of the list to create one (Administrators only). Creating a site asks for a name and a location, by searching an address or picking a point on the map, then opens the new site in Builder.

![Worlds Site Details](/images/Worlds_SiteDetails.png "Worlds Site Details")

The **Site Elements** panel on the right lists everything at the site, grouped by type:

- **Facilities** - buildings at the site, each with its floor and any regions drawn inside it
- **Cameras** - the video sources connected to the space
- **Geofences** - polygons in lat/long space used to trigger workflow logic
- **Points of Interest** - geospatial points that group cameras together

Use **Filter by name or type** to find something quickly, and the eye icon on any group to show or hide that type on the map. Selecting anything in the list zooms the map to it and opens its details on the **Properties** tab.

**Tip:** Each camera in the list has a menu with **View Source**, **Edit Source**, and **View Zones**, which open that camera on the [Cameras page](/sites-and-cameras/cameras-folders-zones) in a new tab.

## Step 2: Edit Site Settings

Click the cog at the top of the Site Elements panel to open the site's settings: **Site Name**, **Longitude**, **Latitude**, and **Site Bounds**. Site bounds control where the map centers and how far it zooms when someone opens this site, in Builder and in [Overwatch](/monitoring/overwatch). Click **Add Site Bounds** and click around the site on the map to draw them, or **Remove Site Bounds** to start over.

**Note:** Storage accounts are assigned to sites from **Settings > Storage Accounts**, by opening the account and choosing its **Assigned Sites**. See [Settings](/settings/overview).

## Step 3: Navigate the Builder Map

The controls in the top right of the map switch between a flat **2D** view and a tilted **3D** view, open the **Layers** menu to show or hide cameras (and their field of view), geofences, points of interest, and facilities, **re-center on the site**, and zoom. In 3D, you can also rotate the map.

To add something new, click the round **+** button in the bottom right of the map and choose:

- **Camera** (or press C) - a video feed placed on the map
- **Point of interest** (or press P) - a named spot with a default view
- **Geofence** (or press G) - an area that triggers events
- **Facility** - a building outline drawn on the map

A hint bar walks you through placing it. Press **Esc** to cancel.

**Note:** A new camera or point of interest is created the moment you click the map, and a new geofence or facility once you finish drawing it, each with a default name you can change.

**Tip:** Scroll to zoom and drag to pan. Getting the right vantage and zoom level makes placing objects and calibrating cameras much easier.

![Worlds Builder Geofence](/images/Worlds_BuilderGeofence.png "Worlds Builder Geofence")

## Step 4: Add and Position a Camera

Add a camera from the **+** menu and click where it sits, or select an existing camera from the Site Elements list. Its details open on the right: **Camera Name**, **Enabled**, **Streaming URL**, **Longitude** and **Latitude**, an optional **Edge Transcoder**, and **Rotation**. **Edit Camera Settings** opens the camera's full settings on the Cameras page.

**Tip:** Get the position as accurate as possible, either by entering exact latitude and longitude or by dragging the camera on the map. Rotation only needs a rough estimate at this stage: drag the direction handle on the camera to turn it (hold Shift to snap to 30°), or type a value. Calibration (Step 5) sets a computed rotation for you afterward, and locks it from manual changes.

Once a camera is placed, confirm it's streaming video before moving on to calibration.

![Worlds Builder Camera](/images/Worlds_BuilderCamera.png "Worlds Builder Camera")

## Step 5: Calibrate the Camera

With a camera selected, find **Calibrations** at the bottom of the right panel and click the plus icon to start a new calibration. This pulls up a recent frame from the camera's video in a panel below the map.

Use **Add Marker** to place matching points on both the map and the video frame. Then drag them into matching positions in both the map and video panels.

**Tip:** Calibration needs at least three markers to run, but an accurate calibration usually takes five to seven. Reference static objects and artifacts, corners, doors, pillars, and place them as accurately as you can - if challenging to locate in the map panel, create a **Facility** and upload a floor plan image to place on the map for reference. Calibration is what lets Worlds translate detections from that camera into real-world locations.

Once all markers are placed, click **Calibrate** to start the calibration job. When it completes, the camera is calibrated, and its detections will begin to carry a lat/long position in addition to the pixel location within the camera frame.

![Worlds Camera Calibration](/images/Worlds_CameraCalibration.png "Worlds Camera Calibration")

## Step 6: Group Cameras with Points of Interest

Add a point of interest from the **+** menu and click where it belongs. It appears on the map as a point you can drag, similar to a camera. In its details, use **Associated Cameras** to choose which cameras belong to it.

**Tip:** Points of interest aren't commonly needed, but they can be valuable when several cameras share a single mount facing different directions, or to group cameras with overlapping views of the same area (such as lots at a construction site, or work areas in a manufacturing facility).

## Step 7: Define Areas with Geofences

Add a geofence from the **+** menu, then click the map to place each corner. Click the first corner again, click **Finish**, or press Enter to close the shape.

To edit a geofence, select it: drag inside the shape to move it, drag a corner to reshape it, click the midpoint of an edge to add a corner, or drag the top handle to rotate it. Its details list every corner's coordinates and its approximate area.

Geofences define specific areas of physical space. They're especially useful alongside calibrated cameras with a wide field of view, common in outdoor settings, letting you build workflow logic that triggers when a detected object does or doesn't intersect the defined area.

**Tip:** Geofences are especially useful if you're integrating GPS devices into your Worlds data.

## Step 8: Add Facilities and Floor Plans

Add a facility from the **+** menu and draw the building's outline on the map, then click the first corner to finish. This creates the facility, typically a building at the site, along with its first floor. A single site can contain multiple facilities if it has multiple buildings.

Select the floor from the Site Elements list (under its facility) to work with its floor plan. Click **Add Floor Plan** to upload an image (jpg or png). The image renders directly onto the map, where you can:

- Drag the corners to rescale and align it
- Use **Freeform** or **Locked** to either warp the image to match points or keep it in proportion
- Fine-tune the exact corner coordinates
- Use the **Blueprint Opacity** slider to see the mapping library's building outline underneath, to confirm alignment

**Tip:** Align the floor plan to the existing building outline in the mapping library as precisely as you can. Once aligned and saved, it becomes a reference for calibrating cameras against interior features (walls, structures, corners, doors), and adds spatial context to cameras, detections, and events elsewhere in the product, including the Floor Plans layer in [Overwatch](/monitoring/overwatch).

Save the floor once you're satisfied with the alignment.

**Note:** If a facility shows **Not on map** in the list, it doesn't have an outline yet. Select it and click **Add outline**, then drag its corners to fit the building.

## Step 9: Set Up Regions

Every facility is also a region, and you can draw smaller regions inside it, so Worlds understands the layout of the space: which building, which zone, which line, which workstation. Regions show up in [Overwatch](/monitoring/overwatch), where users can go inside one to see only the cameras and events that matter there.

Select a facility, or a region inside it, to set:

- **Type** - **Building**, **Zone**, **Line**, or **Workstation**, from largest to smallest, or **Fence** for a boundary outline. A region can sit inside a larger type, while a fence can't sit inside or hold other regions.
- **Parent** - which larger region this one sits inside
- **Enabled** - turn off to hide the region everywhere outside Builder
- **Can enter** - lets users open the region as its own view in Overwatch
- **Colour** and **Fill opacity** - how the region looks on the map

Colour, fill opacity, and Can enter start from the type's defaults, and **Reset** puts any you've changed back.

**Tip:** To let users go inside a building in Overwatch, select its facility, give it a region **Type** such as Building, and make sure **Can enter** is on.

To add a region inside a facility, open its floor and click the plus icon under **Regions**. Worlds adds a new region inside the floor, one type smaller than its parent (a zone inside a building, for example), ready to drag and reshape on the map.

**Note:** A facility needs an outline before you can add regions to it.

## Deleting Elements

Select anything and click its trash icon to delete it, or press Delete or Backspace with a camera, point of interest, geofence, or facility selected. Worlds asks you to confirm first.

- Deleting a camera, point of interest, geofence, facility, or floor can't be undone.
- Deleting a facility or region tells you where any regions inside it will move.
- Deleting a region can be undone.

## Quick Reference

- **Sites** - opens Builder on your favorite or first site; switch, search, star, or create sites from the site name in the top left
- **Access** - Manager and above to view; Administrator and above to edit
- **Saving** - map edits save automatically with Undo and Redo; panel forms have their own Save
- **Site settings** - name, location, and site bounds, from the cog in Site Elements; storage accounts are assigned under Settings
- **Add** - the + button for cameras, points of interest, geofences, and facilities
- **Calibrate** - match markers between map and video (three minimum, five to seven for accuracy) to translate detections to real-world location
- **Points of Interest** - group cameras, mainly for multi-camera mounts or large outdoor sites
- **Geofences** - define areas for workflow triggers, especially useful outdoors
- **Facilities** - building outlines with an aligned floor plan for calibration reference and spatial context
- **Regions** - Building, Zone, Line, Workstation, or Fence, with Can enter for Overwatch
